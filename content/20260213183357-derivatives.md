<div class="definition">

Let \\(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}\\) be open, \\(a \in U\\), and \\(f: U \to \mathbb{R}\\) be a function. We say \\(f\\) is differentiable at \\(a\\) if the following limit exists.

\\[ \lim\_{x\to a} \frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a} \\]

</div>

We say that \\(f\\) is differentiable if the aforementioned limit exists.

<div class="theorem">

if \\(f\\) is differentiable at \\(a\\), then \\(f\\) is continuous at \\(a\\).

</div>

<div class="proof">

\\[
\lim\_{x\to a} f(x) = \lim\_{x\to a} \left(\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a} + f(a) \right) = f'(a)\cdot 0 + f(a)
\\]

</div>
