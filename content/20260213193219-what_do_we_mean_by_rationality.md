These are my notes on the following writing by [What Do We Mean By "Rationality"?](https://www.lesswrong.com/s/5g5TkQTe9rmPS5vvM/p/RcZCwxFiZzE6X7nsv) by Eliezer Yudkowsky.

Yudkowsky defines two types of rationality:

1.  **Epistemic rationality** is systematically improving the accuracy of your beliefs.
2.  **Instrumental rationality** is systematically acheiving your values.

I find these two definitions to be quite consistent with what I imagine ideal rationality to be;
both maximizing the probability of one's beliefs while also acting on them in the most effective way.
Yudkowsky also points out other common ideas of "rationality" such as acting purely through logic
over all feelings. Yudkowsky, however, chooses to lump hunches, perceptions, and wordless intuitions
into the various factors taken into account by Bayesian rationality.
