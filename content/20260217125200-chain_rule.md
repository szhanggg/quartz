If \\(g\\) is [differentiable]({{< relref "20260213183357-derivatives.md" >}}) at \\(a\\) and \\(f\\) is differentiable at \\(g(a)\\),
then \\(f \circ g\\) is differentiable at \\(a\\) and \\((f \circ g)'(a) = f'(g(a))\cdot g'(a)\\)

<div class="proof">

We define
\\[
G(x) = \begin{cases}
\frac{g(x)-g(a)}{x-a} & \text{if } x \neq a \\\\
g'(a) & \text{if } x = a
\end{cases}
\\]
Since \\(g\\) is differentiable at \\(a\\), we know \\(G\\) is continuous at \\(a\\).
Furthermore, let
\\[
F(y) = \begin{cases}
\frac{f(y)-f(g(a))}{y-g(a)} & \text{if } y \neq g(a) \\\\
f'(g(a)) & \text{if } y = g(a)
\end{cases}
\\]
\\(f\\) being differentiable at \\(g(a)\\) implies that \\(F\\) is continuous at \\(a\\).

So
\\[

\begin{aligned}
\lim\_{x\to a}\frac{f(g(x))\_f(g(a))}{x-a} &= \lim\_{x\to a} F(g(x))\cdot G(x) \\\\
&= \lim\_{x\to a} F(g(x)) \cdot \lim\_{x\to a} G(x) \\\\
&= F(g(a)) \cdot g(a) \\\\
&= f'(g(a))\cdot g'(a)
\end{aligned}

\\]

</div>
